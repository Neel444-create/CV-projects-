# Route Assessment — Public Evidence and Commercial Hypotheses

**Status:** preliminary public-source screen, updated 8 November 2026. Not an investment-grade feasibility study.

## Executive view
Mumbai offshore is the strongest first customer-discovery hypothesis because it has identifiable industrial buyers, an established logistics ecosystem and a plausible niche for urgent compact spares. This does **not** establish that the proposed craft is technically feasible, permitted or cheaper. The project has not obtained customer-level shipment weights, cost quotations or vehicle operating costs.

## 1. Chennai–Port Blair
**Public evidence:** The South Andaman district portal states that Chennai has regular ship services to Port Blair, with three to four sailings per month and a voyage time of approximately 50–60 hours in fair weather [S1].

**Commercial hypothesis:** time-sensitive small spares, medical/essential goods and high-value compact cargo may value faster delivery.

**Counterpoint:** Port Blair also has air connectivity. The serviceable niche is only cargo not adequately served by existing air-cargo options.

**Hard gate:** actual vehicle range with reserve, weather diversion and launch/recovery feasibility over the full route. Until this is confirmed, do not treat the route as a viable candidate for a 300 kg service.

## 2. Kochi–Lakshadweep
**Public evidence:** The UT administration says ship passage from Kochi takes 14–18 hours and the flight from Cochin to Agatti takes approximately 1.5 hours [S2]. Its shipping department lists all-weather passenger vessels with cargo capacities of 100–250 tonnes and dedicated cargo vessels of 600–800 tonnes [S3]. Cochin Port's barge EOI describes the islands as 200–400 km from the Kerala coast, varying by island [S4]. The UT administration has also issued a tender for cargo transportation between Kochi and the islands [S10].

**Commercial hypothesis:** urgent, small consignments to a specific island where direct air access is absent or onward transfer is slow.

**Counterpoint:** established bulk cargo capacity is substantial. A small autonomous craft should not be positioned as a replacement for bulk supply.

**Hard gate:** select one island, map true door-to-door route and transfer points, and establish a shipment profile. “Kochi–Lakshadweep” is too broad for final economics.

## 3. Mumbai offshore
**Public evidence:** Mumbai High is described as approximately 160 km west of Mumbai by Sansad TV [S5] and about 165 km west-northwest by the CAG [S6]. Shipping Corporation of India describes offshore logistics using AHTS, platform supply and multipurpose support vessels [S7]. Pawan Hans describes helicopter operations for ONGC offshore personnel and vital supplies [S8]. The maritime administration's offshore safety material identifies offshore installation and support-vessel categories [S9].

**Commercial hypothesis:** urgent, compact, non-hazardous tools and maintenance spares where waiting for the next vessel movement has a measurable operational cost.

**Counterpoint:** the incumbent marine and helicopter ecosystem is mature. The opportunity is a complementary point-to-point urgent service, not bulk displacement.

**Hard gate:** installation-specific operator approval, transfer method, safety-zone permission, operating envelope, insurance and cargo acceptance. No route should be flown into an installation safety zone without the required authorization.

## Provisional ranking
1. **Mumbai offshore — prioritize discovery.** Most focused industrial customer problem; still requires technical, access and cost validation.
2. **Kochi–Lakshadweep — retain as secondary.** Potential niche for island-specific urgent shipments; established cargo shipping and Agatti air access need careful comparison.
3. **Chennai–Port Blair — defer.** Publicly evidenced time gap, but long-route range and weather feasibility are decisive unknowns.

This ranking is a qualitative screening judgement. It is not a numerical market ranking or proof of positive unit economics.

## Competitor / substitute set
- Scheduled cargo and passenger-linked air freight.
- Scheduled passenger ships and dedicated cargo vessels.
- Offshore supply vessels (OSVs), platform supply vessels (PSVs), anchor-handling tug-supply vessels (AHTS) and crew boats.
- Helicopter services for personnel and vital supplies.
- Proposed autonomous sea cargo as a possible urgent small-load layer.

Compare a real shipment against its actual incumbent alternative. Normalize cargo weight, origin/destination, handling, availability, return leg and door-to-door lead time.

## Data still required
- Customer: shipment type, kg per shipment, frequency, value and delay consequence.
- Incumbent: written cost quote/invoice, lead time, service availability and handling.
- Proposed service: actual operating cost, payload, range with reserve, turnaround, weather downtime, insurance and maintenance.
- Access: operator approval, port/installation permission, safety-zone protocol and emergency arrangements.
