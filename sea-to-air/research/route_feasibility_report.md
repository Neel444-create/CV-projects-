# Sea-to-Air: Route Feasibility & Commercial Assessment

**Research status:** Preliminary public-source screening | **Date:** 8 November 2026  
**Positioning:** Independent case study; not affiliated with Waterfly.

## Executive summary

This project screens three Indian route hypotheses for a 300 kg-class autonomous sea-cargo service. Public evidence shows long scheduled sea lead times on island routes, established cargo vessel capacity in Lakshadweep, and an existing offshore logistics ecosystem around Mumbai High.

**Current recommendation:** Prioritize customer discovery for **Mumbai offshore**, focusing on urgent, compact, non-hazardous maintenance spares. This is a discovery priority—not a proven economic winner or technically approved route. Existing offshore support vessels (OSVs/PSVs) and helicopters are established alternatives. A new service must complement these systems and prove value for a narrow shipment category.

No route has demonstrated customer savings yet. Customer-specific shipment weights, incumbent prices, proposed service costs and addressable shipment frequency remain unverified.

## 2. Competitor comparison

- **Scheduled ships and cargo vessels:** Established, high-capacity and appropriate for bulk/planned replenishment. They are not a like-for-like comparator for a single urgent small consignment unless the same shipment can be isolated and costed.
- **Air cargo / helicopters:** Faster where accessible; may be expensive or not available directly to the final endpoint. Use actual shipment quotes, not passenger ticket prices.
- **OSVs / PSVs / crew boats:** The offshore material-logistics backbone. A proposed service should target a narrow urgent niche rather than claim to replace bulk support vessels.
- **Autonomous sea cargo:** Potential fast small-payload option. Actual cost, speed, range, sea-state limits, availability and transfer procedure require operator confirmation.

## 3. Provisional route ranking

The workbook contains a weighted 1–5 screen: urgency 25%, payload fit 20%, customer access 15%, technical feasibility 20%, economics evidence 20%.

Current qualitative order:
1. **Mumbai offshore:** strongest customer-discovery hypothesis; approvals and economics unproven.
2. **Kochi–Lakshadweep:** retain as secondary; choose one island endpoint and one cargo segment.
3. **Chennai–Port Blair:** defer pilot recommendation until vehicle range is confirmed.

Initial scores are judgement-based placeholders. Replace them after interviews and technical review. Do not present the rank as a measured conclusion.

## 4. Unit economics

For each route, obtain a comparable customer shipment and calculate:

- Incumbent cost/kg = incumbent trip cost ÷ actual delivered kg
- Proposed cost/kg = proposed trip cost ÷ actual delivered kg
- Savings/shipment = incumbent trip cost − proposed trip cost
- Savings % = savings ÷ incumbent trip cost
- Time saved = incumbent door-to-door hours − proposed door-to-door hours
- Monthly savings = savings/shipment × eligible monthly shipments

Do not infer costs from passenger fares or addressable demand from total port tonnage. Normalize weight, endpoints, handling, return leg, service availability and delivery scope.

## 5. Pilot concept

**Candidate:** one Mumbai offshore operator, one approved installation/transfer point and one pre-agreed category of non-hazardous compact maintenance spares.

Baseline: current mode, shipment mass, full trip cost, dispatch frequency and door-to-door lead time.

Success measures: successful delivery rate, time saved, cost/kg, payload utilization, customer-confirmed value and repeat-order intent.

Go/no-go requires confirmed technical envelope, range with reserves, weather limits, installation access, permissions, cargo acceptance, emergency procedures and customer economics. A 30-day pilot window may be discussed but is not an approved operating plan.

## 6. Priority next actions

1. Interview 2–3 offshore logistics/procurement stakeholders.
2. Obtain one anonymized shipment profile and incumbent quote.
3. Confirm vehicle payload, range with reserve, sea-state/wind envelope and operating approvals with the operator.
4. Research one specific Lakshadweep island as a secondary benchmark.
5. Populate the workbook with traceable inputs and only then issue a route-specific pilot offer.

## Limitations

Public sources establish connectivity and incumbent service categories, not addressable urgent-cargo volumes, customer-specific freight rates, vehicle operating costs, insurance costs or technical feasibility. This is a discovery-stage commercial screen, not an investment-grade feasibility conclusion.
