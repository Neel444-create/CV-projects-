# Sea-to-Air: Commercial Feasibility & Unit Economics of Autonomous Sea Cargo

A lightweight management-consulting case study evaluating whether autonomous electric sea-cargo craft could create customer value on selected Indian routes.

## Objective
Compare conventional shipping and air freight with an illustrative Waterfly scenario across three candidate route archetypes. Estimate cost per trip, cost per kg, indicative customer savings and relative delivery time, then recommend what should be validated in a customer pilot.

> **Important:** This is an independent student case study, not a Waterfly-approved analysis or commercial proposal. The workbook's route distances, transport costs, cargo loads and times are explicitly illustrative placeholders. They are not verified market rates, Waterfly operating costs, route feasibility findings or customer data. Replace them with sourced evidence before drawing real commercial conclusions.

## Routes in scope

1. Chennai – Port Blair: island logistics and time-sensitive supplies.
2. Kochi – Lakshadweep: essential supplies and equipment for island communities.
3. Mumbai – offshore installations: urgent spare parts and operational supplies.

These are candidate use cases only. Actual feasibility depends on Waterfly's verified range, payload, sea-state envelope, charging, launch/recovery requirements, safety case, permissions and customer operating constraints.

## How to use the model
1. Open the Excel workbook and review **Assumptions & Notes** first.
2. Replace the yellow input cells on **Route Economics** with sourced, like-for-like values.
3. Keep trip-cost scope consistent (origin handling, destination handling, transfers, insurance and other relevant charges).
4. Compare cost per kg only for the cargo actually carried on each service.
5. Treat the Waterfly cost as a scenario input until supported by verified operating data.
6. Use the result as a screening tool, not as a final investment or procurement decision.

## Core formulas
- Cost per kg = trip cost / cargo delivered.
- Savings vs comparator = (comparator cost per kg - Waterfly cost per kg) / comparator cost per kg.
- Time saved = comparator door-to-door time - Waterfly door-to-door time.

## Lightweight decision framework
Score each route on cargo urgency (25%), potential cost savings (25%), cargo demand (20%), route suitability (20%) and pilot accessibility (10%). Do not let a high commercial score override an operational or regulatory blocker.

## Next steps
- Validate route geometry and door-to-door journey times from credible sources.

- Confirm Waterfly performance and operating-cost assumptions with the company; do not infer these from public marketing claims.
- Interview at least one prospective customer or domain expert per route.
- Select one pilot use case with a measurable baseline and agreed success criteria.
